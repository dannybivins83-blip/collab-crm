Most advice about hiring a roofer tells you to "do your research" and "get three quotes." Useful, but it does not tell you what to say when the contractor is standing in your driveway or on the other end of the phone.

These are the questions. They are written the way you would actually ask them. Under each is what you are really protecting yourself from, so you can tell a real answer from a reassuring one.

You do not need to ask all eighteen of every contractor. Ask the ones their quote left unanswered.

## Before they come out

**1. "What's your license number? I'd like to look it up."**
Free, two minutes, and it tells you whether this is a licensed contractor or someone working under somebody else's license. A contractor who hesitates on this question has answered it.

**2. "Can you have your insurer send me certificates for general liability and workers' comp, with my address on them?"**
Sent by the insurer, not forwarded by the contractor. If someone is hurt on your roof and there is no workers' comp, that claim can end up on your homeowner's policy.

## About the work

**3. "Are you tearing the old roof off down to the wood, or going over it? How many layers are up there now?"**
Going over is cheaper and faster and means nobody inspected the deck.

**4. "How many sheets of plywood are included, and what do you charge per sheet after that?"**
The most important money question in the whole list. Without a written per-sheet price, rotted decking gets priced on the day your roof is open.

**5. "Does the price include re-nailing the deck to code?"**
Commonly required, commonly left off the bid, and it will be on the permit.

**6. "What underlayment are you using — brand and product? Synthetic or felt?"**
Different products, different prices. "Underlayment" alone is not an answer.

**7. "Where exactly are you putting ice-and-water shield?"**
You want to hear valleys, eaves, and around every penetration — pipes, skylights, chimney.

**8. "Is all the flashing new, or are you re-using what's there?"**
Where the roof meets a wall or chimney is where new roofs leak. Re-used flashing is invisible on the day the job finishes and expensive two years later.

**9. "Are the pipe boots being replaced?"**
Old rubber collars under a new roof will fail before the warranty runs out.

**10. "Which brand and which exact shingle line?"**
Not the category. The product name. Then look it up.

**11. "Are you using the manufacturer's starter strip and ridge cap, or cut field shingles?"**
Cut shingles are cheaper and faster and can void the wind warranty.

**12. "What ventilation am I getting, and is there enough intake at the soffits to feed it?"**
Exhaust without intake does nothing. Inadequate ventilation is a leading reason manufacturers deny warranty claims.

## About the paperwork

**13. "Are you pulling the permit in your company's name, and is the fee in this price?"**
If you pull it, the county considers you the contractor and you own every defect. This one matters more than it sounds.

**14. "Which manufacturer warranty do I actually get, who registers it, and what do I have to do to keep it valid?"**
The long warranty on the brochure usually needs a certified installer and a registration after the job. A good answer names the specific warranty and says who files it.

**15. "How many years is your workmanship warranty, is it in writing, and does it transfer if I sell?"**
The manufacturer covers the shingle. The contractor covers the installation. Installation is what leaks. If the answer is verbal, it is not a warranty.

## About money and the day

**16. "What's the deposit, when are the other payments due, and what's the final payment tied to?"**
Tie the last payment to the final inspection passing, not to the crew leaving. A large deposit before any material is delivered is the warning sign in this trade.

**17. "If you find something unexpected, do I get a written price before you do the work?"**
This is the clause that prevents a surprise invoice after the roof is already open. Get the answer into the contract.

**18. "How do you protect the landscaping, the AC unit and the pool cage — and do you magnet-sweep when you're done?"**
Nails in the driveway and a damaged condenser are the two most common post-job complaints, and both are avoidable.

## What to do with the answers

Write them down as you go. Then ask each contractor to re-issue the quote with those answers written into it.

That last step is the one most people skip, and it is the one that matters. A verbal "yes, of course all the flashing is new" is worth nothing in four months. The same sentence on the quote is worth exactly what it says.

Two things to watch for. A contractor who answers everything clearly and puts it in writing has shown you how the job will be run. And a contractor who is irritated that you asked has also shown you something — this is the most expensive thing most people buy without a drawing, and asking what you are buying is not an insult.
