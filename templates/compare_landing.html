Why the numbers don't line up

A roof quote is a one-page number sitting on top of about twenty decisions. Contractors who make those decisions explicitly look expensive next to contractors who leave them out — right up until the work starts.

1

What's left off isn't visible

Nothing on a short quote says "re-using your old flashing" or "no allowance for rotten wood." It just isn't there, and there's no line to compare it against.

2

The open items land on you later

"Decking priced as needed" is not a price. Once the old roof is off and the crew is on site, you're negotiating from the worst possible position.

3

Warranties have conditions nobody reads

The long manufacturer warranty usually needs a certified installer and a registration after the job. Miss either and you have the basic one.

The spec sheet

The sheet follows your roof: a tile job gets asked about adhesive foam patterns and underlayment laps, a shingle job about starter strip and ridge cap. For each quote you mark whether the contractor put the line in writing. Anything that isn't a clear yes becomes a question, worded for you to ask on the phone. You'll know which quote is actually cheapest once they all cover the same roof.
